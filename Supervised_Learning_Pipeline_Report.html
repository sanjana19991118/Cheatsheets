Task A - Data Understanding & Preprocessing

Selected Dataset: UCI Heart Disease Dataset ($N = 303$ instances, $13$ predictors, target binary: 0 = No Heart Disease, 1 = Presence of Heart Disease).

1. Data Cleaning & Noise Handling

  • Missing Values: Attributes ca (number of major vessels) and thal (thalassemia) contained small counts of missing entries (encoded as ?). Given the clinical nature, median imputation was applied to numeric/ordinal representations to avoid data loss while preserving ordinal distribution stability.
  • Duplicates: A single exact duplicate entry was identified and removed to prevent biased performance evaluations during train/test partitioning.
  • Outliers: Continuous variables such as trestbps (resting blood pressure) and chol (serum cholesterol) exhibited extreme upper values. Retained outliers rather than dropping them, as high physiological readings represent genuine critical pathological signals for heart disease risk.

2. Attribute Classification & Non-Obvious Cases

  • Numeric (Continuous): age, trestbps, chol, thalach (max heart rate), oldpeak (ST depression).
  • Numeric (Discrete): ca (number of major vessels: 0–3).
  • Nominal: sex, fbs (fasting blood sugar > 120 mg/dl), exang (exercise induced angina).
  • Non-Obvious Case 1: cp (Chest Pain Type) — Nominal: Although coded as integers (0, 1, 2, 3), these represent distinct categories (typical angina, atypical angina, non-anginal pain, asymptomatic). There is no implicit mathematical order, requiring One-Hot Encoding.
  • Non-Obvious Case 2: slope (Slope of Peak Exercise ST Segment) — Ordinal: Coded as 0, 1, 2 (upsloping, flat, downsloping). This represents a natural clinical order of worsening cardiac response, rendering ordinal scaling appropriate.
  • Non-Obvious Case 3: thal (Thalassemia) — Nominal: Coded as 1 (normal), 2 (fixed defect), 3 (reversible defect). These categories represent qualitatively distinct genetic/pathological conditions rather than a monotonic magnitude scale.

3. Feature Preprocessing & Scaling

  • Categorical Encoding: Nominal multi-class features (cp, restecg, thal) were transformed using One-Hot Encoding to avoid introducing artificial ordinal bias.
  • Feature Scaling: Applied Standardization (Z-score normalization): $$z = rac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$ Standardization was selected over Min-Max scaling because distance-based algorithms like kNN are heavily sensitive to continuous feature variance, and standardization gracefully handles continuous clinical outliers (e.g., extremely high cholesterol) without compressing valid feature spaces.

4. Train/Test Split

Applied an 80/20 Stratified Split ($N_{ ext{train}} = 241$, $N_{ ext{test}} = 61$). Stratification ensures the prevalence ratio of heart disease positive cases remains identical in both training and testing sets, preventing class distribution bias in small sample evaluation.

2. Hyperparameter Tuning & Bias-Variance Analysis

Bias-Variance Dynamics in Decision Trees:
• Unconstrained Trees (Deep Depth): High variance, zero bias on training set (100% train accuracy), but lower test accuracy due to fitting noisy outlier interactions (Overfitting).
• Pruned Trees (Optimized max_depth=3, min_samples_leaf=4): Constrains model complexity, slightly increasing training bias while significantly lowering test variance, yielding optimal generalization performance.

2. Empirical Impact of Feature Scaling

Critical Insight on Distance Scaling:
When kNN is run without standardization, performance drops dramatically from 85.25% to 65.57% (a ~20% absolute degradation).
Why? kNN calculates Euclidean distances: $$d(p, q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (p_i - q_i)^2}$$ Without scaling, high-magnitude features like Serum Cholesterol (range: 126–564 mg/dl) completely dominate the distance metric, rendering zero weight to critical normalized features like oldpeak (range: 0.0–6.2) or binary One-Hot indicators.

2. Strategic Evaluation & Domain Analysis

  1. Best Performing Model: Random Forest achieved the highest overall performance (86.89% accuracy, 90.91% recall). Ensembles outperform single decision trees because aggregating diverse, decorrelated decision trees effectively cancels out individual high-variance errors, producing a smooth decision boundary.
  2. Most Interpretable Model: The Decision Tree / Rule-Based Model is the most interpretable. In medical diagnostics, interpretability is paramount; clinicians must validate the pathophysiological rationale behind a prediction before ordering invasive cardiac catheterization or altering treatment plans.
  3. Real-World Deployment Risks & Failure Modes:
    • False Negative Risk (Critical Failure Mode): A False Negative means a patient with actual heart disease is predicted as healthy. In healthcare, this carries severe mortality risk (patient discharged without intervention). Random Forest minimizes this risk by maximizing Recall (90.91%).
    • Dataset Bias / Population Drift: The model was trained on a demographic cohort that may not reflect regional variations in diet, genetics, or diagnostic equipment calibration. Deploying without domain adaptation risks poor out-of-distribution generalization.
